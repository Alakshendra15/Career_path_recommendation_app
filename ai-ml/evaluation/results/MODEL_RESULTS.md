# Trained model results

Generated by `training/train.py`, `evaluation/blend_sweep.py` and `evaluation/model_report.py`.

## Data
- **Real labelled postings:** 583,378 from `lukebarousse/data_jobs` (Apache-2.0, Google job postings 2023), 101,503 unique (skill set, career) rows. Careers with a real title label: cloud_engineer, data_analyst, data_engineer, data_scientist, ml_engineer.
- **Weak labels:** 16,832 'Software Engineer' postings labelled python_developer / web_developer from their skills (not a title, so never counted in accuracy) plus 5,993 synthetic profiles from the knowledge base.
- Train/validation/test are split by skill-set hash, so the same skill set never appears on both sides.

## Held-out accuracy on real postings (5 real careers)
| | Top-1 | Top-3 |
|---|---|---|
| Always guess the biggest class | 38.0% | - |
| Rule-based recommender (same postings) | 50.9% | 73.4% |
| Logistic regression alone (same postings) | 53.4% | 86.4% |
| Logistic regression, full test fold | 54.2% | 86.2% |

Macro-F1 0.44. Per-career recall: cloud_engineer 54.0%, data_analyst 71.1%, data_engineer 53.1%, data_scientist 37.6%, ml_engineer 45.2%.

Job-title labels are noisy: a 'Data Analyst' and a 'Data Scientist' posting can list identical skills, so top-1 has a low ceiling and top-3 is the fairer headline.

## Blending rules and model (`ML_WEIGHT`)
`final = (1 - w) * rule_score + w * model_probability`. Fold 8 tunes w, fold 9 reports it; the personas are a student-style check.

Chosen: **w = 0.5**. w = 0 is the old rule-only system, w = 1 is the model alone.

## Limitations
- Postings are US job ads, not student profiles: skills are what employers ask for, not what a student has.
- Python Developer and Web Developer have no real title labels, so their accuracy is only checked on the hand-written personas.
- Interests are not in the posting data; they enter only through the rule-based part.
- The 52 personas are small and were written by the project team.
